# Curating data-appropriate visual alternatives

The purpose is to show researchers useful options they may not already know, rendered with their actual data. Count usable choices for the current dataset, not a marketing total of chart names.

For each new family, record required variable types, paired/repeated structure, raw-data versus summary-data needs, supported statistical meaning, rendering recipe, and bilingual explanations of the question answered and information lost or gained. Validate the renderer and real-data preview before adding it to recommendations. A summary-only dataset cannot support invented raw points, violin distributions, or individual trajectories. Aesthetic preference never overrides eligibility.

Separate chart family from canvas aspect ratio and composite slot geometry. Retain common group colours, scientific annotations, and manual edits where their identity remains valid. Preview the panel in its intended composite position; provide wide and tall redraws only where implemented. Store assets once and keep state history separately.

Primary reference collections for subsequent curation:

- Seaborn distributions: https://seaborn.pydata.org/tutorial/distributions.html
- Seaborn categorical comparisons: https://seaborn.pydata.org/tutorial/categorical.html
- Matplotlib gallery: https://matplotlib.org/stable/gallery/
- Plotly statistical charts: https://plotly.com/python/statistical-charts/
- Vega examples: https://vega.github.io/vega/examples/
- Vega-Lite documentation: https://vega.github.io/vega-lite/docs/

Consult the relevant primary documentation when adding a recipe; collection membership alone is not evidence of suitability. Current discovery metadata adds bilingual names and explanations to existing upstream renderers, not new analysis capabilities. No continuous background harvesting or account-based recommendation service is implemented.
